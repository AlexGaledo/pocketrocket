# Toki design critique, 2026-11-21

Scope: the marketing site (`packages/site`, toki-hub.vercel.app) and the app UI (`packages/web`, also the Tauri desktop shell).
Method: both run locally against a throwaway hub seeded with demo bots ("Ada", "Milo", a "Weekend plans" room); no real
user data. Captured at 1280x800 and 390x844, light and dark. Reviewed against the impeccable craft floor and Emil
Kowalski's motion rules. Screenshots: `docs/design/before/` (emails masked).

Owner's brief, in one line: *Toki is a cozy home for your team of AI helpers.* Cream paper, espresso ink, one terracotta
accent, Nunito headings, the sitting cat.

## Ranked findings

### 1. The site oversells the plumbing and undersells the feeling (site, high)

- It names models and versions three times: "Sonnet 5, Opus 5, Fable 5.1 or Haiku 4.5, per bot", and a whole FAQ entry
  ranking them by price ("Which model is cheapest?"). Model line-ups change every few months; the page dates itself and
  reads like a spec sheet.
- It admits a roadmap ("v1 ships Claude; Codex, OpenCode and Grok adapters are next"). That is a promise the page cannot
  keep current and invites "so it doesn't work with X yet".
- It leaks internals: `%APPDATA%\com.pocketrocket.app` (the old product name, in the third install step), "systemd
  service with a virtual desktop", "SSH tunnel", "Supabase" named twice, `pnpm install && pnpm dev` as step one of a
  Windows download story.
- Nothing on the page says how it *feels* to use Toki. Every section is a list of capabilities.

### 2. Old voice still on the surface (site + app, high)

"Your pocket fleet of AI agents" is still the site's H1, its `<title>` description, the OG/Twitter cards, the 404 page
("drifted out of orbit", an orbit ring with a moon), the app's setup wizard and Settings > About. "Everything a fleet
needs", "fleet changes". The rebrand changed the name and the colours but kept the PocketRocket metaphor in the words.

### 3. Template layout (site, high)

Centred section heading + same-size card grid, five times over: a hero with an icon above the H1 and a small screenshot to
the right, then "Your model" (one card), "Everything a fleet needs" (six identical cards), "How it works" (three identical
cards), "Run on a server" (one card), FAQ, newsletter card. Uniform 56px section rhythm, uniform 20px radii, uniform
shadow. Radial gradient washes in two corners of the background. Nothing on the page could not belong to any other
SaaS landing page; the cat appears only as a logo.

The hero screenshot is the single best asset on the page and it is shown at ~460px wide, too small to read anything in it.

### 4. Chrome competing with content (site, medium)

- A floating "Get help" pill sits over the content mid-page at desktop width (visible over the "Your model" section in the
  capture) and a back-to-top button joins it once scrolled.
- A 3px scroll-progress bar across the top: decoration on a page this short.
- The theme toggle cycles through three states with one icon and is the only control in the bar at phone width besides
  the menu.

### 5. Self-deprecating / anxious install copy (site, medium)

"The installer is unsigned, so SmartScreen may warn", "or build from source if you'd rather skip that". The note is
needed, but it is phrased as an apology and buried in the FAQ. It belongs next to the download button, said calmly.

### 6. App: motion has no press or entrance language (app, medium)

The app deliberately limits motion to status effects, which is right for a tool used all day, but it skips the cheap,
purposeful ones:

| Before | After | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Buttons have hover only, no `:active` | `scale(0.97)` on press, 150ms ease-out | Press feedback confirms the click landed |
| Dialogs, menus and popovers appear in one frame | 150-200ms fade + `scale(0.97)` from the trigger's transform-origin; dialogs from centre; phone sheets slide up | Occasional surfaces; appearing from nothing feels broken |
| Exit = enter speed (n/a, no exit) | Exit 120ms, faster than enter | System response should be quicker than the user's decision |
| Toasts pop in | Slide/fade in from the bottom edge they live on | Spatial consistency |
| Setup-wizard step dots use `transition-all` | `transition-[width,background-color]` | Name the properties |
| Tooltips: no animation | unchanged | Seen tens of times a day; instant is correct |

All of it respects `prefers-reduced-motion` (opacity only, no movement).

### 7. App: copy in onboarding and empty states (app, medium)

- Setup welcome: "Your pocket fleet of AI agents" / "Friendly bots that work for you. You okay anything risky." The
  second line is good; the first is the old brand.
- First-bot templates show raw tool names in monospace chips (`WebSearch`, `WebFetch`, `Bash`) and system-prompt-like
  titles. Implementation detail in a first-run screen.
- Empty chat: "All quiet here. Say hi whenever you're ready." in 14px muted text floating at the top of an empty pane;
  fine words, no presence.
- Settings > About repeats the old tagline.
- Group chat name placeholder is `launch-plan`.

### 8. App: a few colours outside the token system (app, low)

- Search highlight is hard-coded amber `#f5b53d` / `#1a1200` in both `SearchDialog.tsx` and `::highlight(search)`.
- Modal scrims are `bg-black/30` / `bg-black/60`: neutral black over a warm room. They should be warm espresso.
- `botCharacter.tsx` and the Google sign-in glyph keep their own colours on purpose (character art and a third-party
  brand mark); not changed.

## Left alone, on purpose

- **Model picker labels** ("Sonnet 5 (default)", "Opus 5", "Haiku 4.5") and the plan-limit names ("weekly Opus limit")
  come from `packages/shared/src/models.ts` and `lib/limits.ts` and are what the provider itself calls them; changing
  them would touch shared config the hub also uses. They only appear where you pick or monitor a model, which is where a
  real name is useful.
- The built-in support bot's handle `@pocketrocket-support` is created by the hub and stored in user data; renaming it is a
  migration, not a copy change.
- Internal identifiers (`@pocketrocket/*` packages, `pocketrocket.*` storage keys) are not user-visible.
- The site has no separate privacy or terms pages on main; the subscribe form's privacy note and the MIT licence link
  stay.
